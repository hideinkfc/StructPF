01

Can LLMs design Productive Failure exercises?

What we did, why we did it, what we found, and what remains. Status as of 13 October 2026.

Productive Failure (PF): students attempt an ill-structured problem before they are taught the method. This deck summarizes the work; exact protocols and caveats are in the linked records.

02

Why this question matters

A fluent word problem is not yet a PF exercise.

What PF needs

Students can start from what they know, several sensible approaches are possible, some approaches fail usefully, and the key idea stays for them to find.

What we feared

An LLM can write a polished story that reduces to one procedure, or announces the answer, while looking ready for a classroom.

What we needed

Evidence that separates real PF properties from fluency, plus honest limits on what model judges can show.

03

Five research questions and where they stand

QuestionCurrent answer
RQ1 Do well-rated LLM tasks contain the PF mechanism?Inconclusive. Model reviews raised candidate flaws, but the paired audit did not corroborate them.
RQ2 Can LLMs anticipate what students will bring?Partly. Mistake pathways trade realism against variety; generated method lists recalled 20–27% of frequent real errors.
RQ3 Does explicit decomposition beat strong direct prompting?Not in general. Equal results in the expert-direction comparison, with about nine times the requests.
RQ4 Can simulated students improve task design?Diagnostic only. Feedback did not reliably beat strong review.
RQ5 Does curriculum grounding improve PF tasks?Pilot inconclusive. Component gains appeared, but the primary outcome was almost entirely unresolved.

Each answer reflects the strength of evidence reached. Model judgments and simulated attempts are not human ratings or student outcomes.

Support by checklist part, out of 54 tasks

Blue: supported. Red: agreed concern (none). Grey: unknown or disputed; unknown is not failure. These are model reviews, not human ratings.

What the null means

Sixteen single-judge concerns remain disputed candidates; the strict rule did not confirm a broad hidden-flaw pattern.

What it does not mean

It does not validate the tasks. One judge was lenient, controls were uneven, and 44 of 54 checklist outcomes stayed unknown.

11

Grounding result: directional gains, unresolved primary outcome

Supported outputs out of 20 per condition

Supported means both model auditors agreed and quoted the task. Unknown is not a failure; mathematical support was unresolved for 53 of 60 tasks.

Curriculum

Higher support on target relevance and useful contrast. One task had all five properties supported, versus none without references; this is not evidence of a general gain.

Learning Commons

Choice support rose slightly, but target relevance, distinct ideas and useful contrast fell. No consistent incremental benefit.
