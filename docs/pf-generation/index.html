Productive Failure / October 10, 2026

Design and check. Then write the task.

The default pipeline selects a design brief, proposes given data and conditions, calculates what it can, and retains model judgments and uncertainty. It repairs numerical failures within a bounded budget, then writes with fixed data and unresolved findings rather than stopping on quality concerns. Useful approaches can be partial or successful; a numerical trap is not mandatory.

03 / Illustrative numerical example

Group sizes change the mean.

In this teaching example, unequal class sizes make the unweighted mean differ from the mean across students. This optional contrast is not a universal PF requirement or a measured generation result.

Mean across individual speeds

Each student travels for one hour at their own stated speed. We compare the mean distance per student, expressed as a mean speed, not the speed of a physically merged group.

mean speed = sum of individual speeds / students
Illustrative prompt, not a validated PF task
Class A has 4 students, each travelling at 12 km/h for one hour. Class B has 12 students, each travelling at 4 km/h for one hour. Across all 16 students, what is the mean distance travelled per student? Show and explain your reasoning. Numbers below illustrate arithmetic; they do not show student attempts.

04 / Where MCQ fits

MCQ pathways are hypotheses, not student evidence.

The multiple-choice question (MCQ) study offers inspectable error-path hypotheses. It is not the primary source of prior knowledge or a completed student-response evaluation of PF tasks.

Useful jobs around generation

MCQ instrument Candidate errors and structured paths; reference alignment is secondary.
→
Future behavioral probes Test possible responses; model predictions still need validation.
Current PF design Curriculum-grounded resources → selected brief → given data and conditions.
→
Short PF prompt Fixed givens and teacher-only checks, including nonblocking uncertainty.

The boundary to protect

Random misconception labels produced high textual pathway diversity but lower plausibility and reference alignment. That is a warning against treating forced variety as student behavior.

MCQ output is not pasted into the PF writer. A better distractor score is not evidence of a better PF exercise.

Remaining design concerns: speed asks about a fixed journey, not an unknown handover point; consistency discloses equal averages and extremes; division gives allocation values. No simulated or real student approaches or learning outcomes were observed.
